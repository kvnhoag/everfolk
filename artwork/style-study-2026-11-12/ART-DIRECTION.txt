WHAT CHIAROSCURO MEANS FOR EVERFOLK
Lighting carries attention: eyes first, expression second, hands or signature
object third. Preserve facial asymmetry and distinctive proportions.
Use large, deliberate shadow shapes and quiet backgrounds. Avoid glossy skin,
uniform beauty, decorative noise and the same heroic expression on everyone.
Deep shadows should retain readable skin and fabric information across skin
tones and at the displayed size. Dramatic lighting can also support warmth,
curiosity, fatigue, humour or unease; every person need not look grim.
Keep the approved Printed Folktale interface, Eczar lettering and jade/cream/
clay brand relationship as the surrounding context for this exploration.

REFERENCE → AURA → STYLE → LAYERS → PIXEL FINISH → SIZE CHECK
1. Establish who, when and where. Gather primary object/portrait references,
   costume and material evidence, and setting references separately. Record
   source links and which choices are supported, inferred or invented.
2. Write an aura brief: three emotional traits, one tension, posture, gaze,
   silhouette, light, palette and one meaningful object. Aura is an art
   direction interpretation, not a claim to know a person's psychology.
3. Translate that brief into the shared chiaroscuro language. Preserve the
   person's distinctive face and bearing; avoid dressing one generic face
   in a succession of costumes.
4. Approve an identity master and pose. Construct transparent components
   around that fixed identity with shared dimensions, anchors and lighting.
5. Composite in ArtCraft, then apply a reproducible pixel finish. Simplify
   colour and edge noise deliberately. Fix weak anatomy and expressions
   before pixelation; the filter cannot repair those defects.
6. Review at actual portrait, event and card display sizes, plus a reduced
   thumbnail. Clean facial pixel clusters and silhouette edges after review.

PROPOSED AURA PILOT CAST — ART DIRECTION INTERPRETATIONS
Alexander: contained authority, urgency, outward ambition; resolve versus
fatigue. Lifted gaze, off-centre hair silhouette, controlled turn of the neck.
Aristotle: observation, intellectual weight, measured scepticism; certainty
versus curiosity. Attentive eyes and a grounded conversational gesture.
Euclid: precision, patience, clarity; rigor versus discovery. Quiet alignment
and a constructive gesture. His face must be an explicitly imagined design.
Fictional female artisan: capability, warmth, lived experience; pride versus
exhaustion. Economical gestures, practical clothing and a distinctive face.
Fictional navigator: endurance, judgement, alertness; confidence versus risk.
Weathered features, a stable silhouette and a gaze attentive to distance.
These are proposed future tests, not additional assets created in this study.

ONE LANGUAGE, THREE ASSET FAMILIES
Portrait: largest attention budget on eyes, brows, mouth, face proportions
and hair silhouette. Clothing changes must preserve the approved identity.
Event scene: communicate the action and emotional relationship first. Use
light, spacing and foreground overlap to guide the eye. Maintain the same
face and costume system, with reusable settings and props where practical.
TCG/NFT artwork: give the figure a strong iconic composition using the same
identity and rendering language. Keep frame, text, badges and rarity finish
outside the artwork's pixel filter and independently editable. Layout and
components will be decided separately. Nothing is minted by this study.

This order is a starting point: individual poses may require front/back
garment splits or hands on both sides of an object. A single universal layer
order is insufficient for every costume and pose.
Every replaceable part needs common canvas size, registration anchors,
occlusion masks and a lighting contract. Generate neutral continuous body
coverage beneath garments rather than relying on holes hidden by one outfit.
Identity stays fixed when clothing or rarity changes. Rarity changes should
not silently regenerate the face or change the emotional character.
Keep figure-local modelling and shadows with the relevant components; apply
the shared finish after assembly so parts share a pixel grid and colour grade.
Backgrounds should not be baked into transparent character masters. Preserve
edge alpha without a dark fringe. Keep source components before finishing.

PIXEL FINISH AND ARTCRAFT — ORIGINAL CAPABILITY STUDY
Use EffectCraft Mosaic with sharpColors to compare controlled block scales.
Posterize changes channel levels; it is not a fixed-size colour palette.
No dedicated palette quantization/dither or nearest-neighbour layer sampler
was found in the inspected EffectCraft catalog. Do not describe Mosaic as
hand-authored pixel art or assume registry support guarantees a good render.
Use a consistent pixel density within each asset family, with grid counts
derived from the dimensions; do not copy identical block counts to a small
portrait and a wide event scene. Judge eyes and expression before approving.
Existing 32×40 portraits and 240×112 banners remain runtime constraints for
direct replacements. Larger card art requires its own export contract.
Higher-detail artwork does not automatically remain legible at 32×40.

HISTORICAL BOUNDARIES
The game starts near Rhakotis in 350 BCE, with Alexandria developing after
331 BCE. Do not insert completed later landmarks into early dated events.
Ancient portraits are representations, often idealised and sometimes later
copies. Do not present the generated Alexander face as an exact likeness.
Fayum portraits, early modern paintings/prints and later Roman mosaics are
medium/composition references only; their costumes are not automatically
evidence for fourth-century BCE Alexandria.
