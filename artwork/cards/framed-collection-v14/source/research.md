# Everfolk v14 card copy and historical checks

Prepared 13 November 2026. Card copy is English and gender-neutral. The 13 main portraits retain their historical identities. The 52 contributor roles are fictional game roles, each represented by male and female art. Their paragraphs describe the profession, responsibility, and value of that role; they do not claim that any depicted contributor is an independently attested ancient person.

The titles and prose are editorial interpretations for a historical game. Canonical existing role titles are preserved in `title`; concise card-face versions appear in `displayTitle`. Historical biographies are retrospective summaries of a person's career, not evidence of a player's resolved promotion milestone.

## Main biographies and evidence boundaries

- **Alexander:** [Metropolitan Museum of Art, The Rise of Macedon and the Conquests of Alexander the Great](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-rise-of-macedonia-and-the-conquests-of-alexander-the-great). Supports reign, the Persian conquest, eastward campaigns, and the Hellenistic legacy. Avoids disputed death date and claims of perfect military invincibility.
- **Aristotle:** [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Aristotle](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle/) and [Aristotle's Biology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-biology/). Supports scope of inquiry and Lyceum setting. The prose does not claim modern scientific methods in their present form or treat every work in the transmitted corpus as securely authentic.
- **Euclid:** [MacTutor, University of St Andrews](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Euclid/) and [Princeton University Press, Who was Euclid?](https://press.princeton.edu/ideas/who-was-euclid). Euclid's personal biography is poorly known. The text uses “associated with Alexandria,” does not invent birth/death dates or a documented royal appointment, and emphasises the *Elements* and its method rather than claiming he invented all of its mathematics.
- **Lysippos:** [Getty ULAN record](https://www.getty.edu/vow/ULANFullDisplay?find=&nation=&role=&subjectid=500012332) and [Getty, Power and Pathos checklist](https://www.getty.edu/art/exhibitions/power_pathos/BRONZES_EXHIBITION_CHECKLIST.pdf). Supports bronze practice, Sikyon, and association with Alexander. No surviving bronze is presented as an uncontested original by him; no generated portrait is labelled archaeological evidence.
- **Pytheas:** [Oxford Dictionary of National Biography](https://academic.oup.com/oxford-dictionary-of-national-biography/reference/63486/reference-article-abstract/581793426) and [Wiley Encyclopedia of Ancient History](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781444338386.wbeah21281.pub2). The original account is lost; route, the identity of Thule, exact dates, and practical voyage arrangements are uncertain. Card prose does not identify Thule with a particular modern country or claim a precisely reconstructed itinerary.
- **Menander:** [Oxford Classical Dictionary](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/549651704?login=false) and [Dickinson College Commentaries, author and transmission](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/menander-epitrepontes/intro/intro). Supports New Comedy, Athenian theatre, character, and later influence. The words about recognisable motives and domestic relationships are a concise literary interpretation, not a quotation.
- **Demetrius:** [Oxford Classical Dictionary](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/548753179) and [Oxford University Press, The Great Siege of Rhodes](https://academic.oup.com/book/40551/chapter-abstract/347966363). Historical Rhodes siege ended through negotiation, not capture. V14 biography describes the historical branch. It does not silently claim the alternate-history victory that would enable a gameplay Legendary promotion.
- **Theophrastus:** [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/theophrastus/). Uses colleague and successor rather than reducing his relationship with Aristotle to an uncomplicated teacher–pupil relation. The botanical studies were broad comparative investigations; artwork and contributor scenes are plausible inventions.
- **Zeno:** [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Stoicism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/stoicism/). Supports the Painted Porch, foundation around 300 BCE, and centrality of reason and virtue. Biography is retrospective; no claim that a school was already founded at a game date in 323 BCE. This does not resolve a player's provisional school-established promotion gate.
- **Ptolemy:** [University College London, Digital Egypt, Ptolemy I](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt/chronology/ptolemyi.html). Supports transition from governor to independent king. Dynasty/royal-capital wording is a retrospective career summary. The “Soter” historical identity follows existing catalogue naming, not a newly awarded in-game epithet. No claim that the Library was solely founded or personally built by him.
- **Cleomenes:** [Oxford Classical Dictionary](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/548753033) and [Oxford University Press, Money and Prices in the Papyri](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/43505/chapter/364128623). Supports appointment and fiscal/grain powers; preserves the hostile ancient accusations as accusations. Detailed contemporary administration remains poorly documented. Descriptions of honest auditors and careful porters are game archetypes, not proof that Cleomenes' actual administration was benevolent.
- **Nearchus:** [Arrian, Indica, Fordham Ancient History Sourcebook](https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/ancient/arrian-bookVIII-India.asp) and [Livius, Arrian on the voyage home](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/arrian/anabasis/nearchus-voyage-home/). Primary ancient narrative supports fleet command and coasting voyage from the Indus towards the Persian Gulf. “Pilot of the Indus Fleet” is a fictional contributor role, not an identification of a historical pilot. Never give the new art a magnetic compass, sextant, or modern navigation chart.
- **Eumenes:** [Oxford Classical Dictionary](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/548934031?login=false). Supports service as secretary to Philip and Alexander and subsequent military command. The card does not conflate him with a later king of Pergamon or imply royal Macedonian birth.

## Icons

The production symbols are actual professionally drawn assets, not the original hand-drawn placeholders. Twelve come from Game-icons.net by Lorc and Delapouite under CC BY 3.0; Euclid uses Google Material Icons architecture (a drafting compass) under Apache 2.0. Unmodified SVGs, creator/source URLs, licenses and SHA256 values are retained in source/icons/ and source/icon-sources.json. The authoring pipeline removes only the full-canvas background and tints the glyph to the rarity palette. The public collection links its human-readable credits page.

These are design associations, not attested personal insignia. Crossed swords identify Alexander; an unfurled scroll Aristotle; a drafting compass Euclid; a chisel Lysippos; a galley Pytheas; drama masks Menander; a siege tower Demetrius; bundled herbs Theophrastus; a Greek temple Zeno; an Egyptian double crown Ptolemy; bundled grain Cleomenes; a trireme Nearchus; and a tied scroll Eumenes. No magnetic compass or feather quill was introduced.

## Rarity and numbering

V13 main ranks are retained: five Legendary and eight Epic, with Nearchus/Eumenes Epic styling remaining an editorial default. Each figure now has Common, Uncommon, Rare and Epic contributor role art, with two gender variants per role. The six additional lower-tier Nearchus/Eumenes roles are new editorial art categories, not claims that gameplay has already implemented those tiers.

Numbered footer strings such as `001/025` are visual examples requested by the user. They do not attest token issuance. Common and Uncommon remain unlimited under current policy; Rare uses `001/100`, Epic `001/025`, and Legendary `001/010`.

No sources are quoted verbatim. The card biographies paraphrase only the modest factual claims listed above. No PubMed or Crossref workflows were used.
