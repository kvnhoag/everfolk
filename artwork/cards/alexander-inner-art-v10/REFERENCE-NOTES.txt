ARMOUR — ACTUAL SURVIVING OBJECT
Iron cuirass with gold decoration from Aigai / Vergina Tomb II, fourth century
BCE, traditionally attributed to Philip II. Used as a regional/period model,
not identified here as Alexander's own armour. Its construction informs the
torso plates, rigid shoulder flaps, geometric gold borders and lion-head/ring
fastenings. In-use surface, fit to a raised-arm rider, hidden construction,
underclothes and the lower pteruges are interpretations. The gold-edged lower
strips are a visual reconstruction, not a documented surviving part of this
cuirass. The generated torso is not an exact dimensional reproduction.

SWORD — ACTUAL MUSEUM OBJECT, EARLIER TYPE COMPARISON
Iron xiphos with gold-sheet decoration (MTh 18800), Archaeological Museum of
Thessaloniki; foreground sword in the photograph. The photographed label gives
grave goods from Nea Philadelpheia, tombs 39 and 67, late sixth century BCE.
This earlier object is used only for the straight double-edged sword's form,
small transverse guard, and coherent axial construction, not as proof of the
precise sword worn by Alexander. Reconstructed serviceable metal, grip material,
and visibly increased blade length are artistic choices. The requested longer
blade is an intentional enlargement, not the dimensions of this museum sword.
