EVERFOLK / ALEXANDER / PAINTERLY STUDY V3

Purpose
Test a more realistic model of flesh and selective, form-following brushwork
while retaining the mounted Alexander composition and clear gaze. This is an
exploratory visual treatment, not a final card specification.

Painting references (rendering, not sitter identity)
Rembrandt van Rijn, Self-Portrait, 1659. National Gallery of Art.
https://www.nga.gov/artworks/79-self-portrait
https://www.nga.gov/research/publications/dutch-paintings-seventeenth-century/rembrandt-van-rijn-self-portrait-1659
Local: references/rembrandt-self-portrait-1659.jpg (official public-domain image).

Diego Velazquez, Juan de Pareja, 1650. The Metropolitan Museum of Art.
https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/437869
Local: references/velazquez-juan-de-pareja.jpg (official public-domain image).

Art-direction interpretation
Warm ochre/rose highlights, cool gray-brown halftones, connected facial planes,
selective sharp accents at the eyes and nose, lost edges in deep shadow.
Directional varied strokes for skin, broad cloth masses and sparse fold accents.
The references do not establish Alexander's appearance or ancient clothing.

Inherited pose and staging
Composition preserved from alexander-reference-v2: Naples mounted bronze
(Getty conservation publication), Zuccaro's Alexander and Bucephalus (NGA),
and phalanx staging from Warner Bros' Alexander production material.
See ../alexander-reference-v2/REFERENCE-NOTES.txt for source details.
This imagined Alexander is not a verified reconstruction or an actor likeness.

Production and provenance
The base was created with the built-in image generation/editing tool, using
the v2 clean mounted illustration and the two museum painting references.
Exact prompt: source/generation-prompt.txt.
Generated base: source/alexander-painterly-base.png.
Actual selected ArtCraft brush marks are authored separately over that base;
the brush ledger, native painting document and transparent overlay preserve
their contribution. The illustration is not wholly painted by hand.
Brushwork is applied before PixelOE, not as an all-over texture filter.
Completed pass: 30 native brush.freehand strokes across four paint layers.
The overpaint is intentionally subtle: 8,143 source pixels changed (about
0.52% of the canvas); the museum-guided generated base supplies the main
rendering. The brush master reopened with all 30 marks and reproduced its
transparent brush export exactly. See qa/brush-proof.json and
qa/native-brush-reopen.json. Actual stroke ledger:
source/ArtCraft-brush-stroke-ledger.json.

Pixel finishing and assembly
source/pixelate-card.py uses the installed isolated PixelOE 1.0.0 CPU runtime.
Logical image grid 420 x 525, exact nearest-neighbour 2x to 840 x 1050.
No palette reduction; facial colours and pupils are retained for legibility.
The ArtCraft card layout is 900 x 1260 with independently editable frame,
type, emblem, rarity and footer. The portrait remains a flattened artwork.
Source painting brush layers are independently editable; clothing variants
are not yet a fully separable character rig.
Native raster export slightly interpolates pixel edges, so the final card PNG
composites the original pixel image at exact 100% under exported native type
and frame layers. The final pixel check is in qa/final-crisp-export.json.
Completed export: 765,234 unobscured portrait pixels checked with no differing
RGB channels. Live card fonts resolved exactly and native reopening reproduced
the renderer's export. Final visual review included the 450 x 630 thumbnail.

Scope
One bounded generated base and a focused brush overpaint, followed by pixel
finishing. A full manually authored repaint was intentionally outside this
proof because it would require substantially more drawing operations/tokens.
No exact token-cost claim is made. Rarity, card number and flavour are concepts.
