Evidence and uncertainty
The Louvre dates this Roman work to 1-50 CE and relates it to a late-fourth-
century BCE Lysippos original. Its inscription identifies Alexander, son of
Philip, Macedonian. The nose was reconstructed; chips on brows/lips were
completed; the surface is worn. These features limit a literal reconstruction.
This is an ancient portrait reference, not a verified photograph-like likeness.

Hair context also checked against museum descriptions
https://www.theacropolismuseum.gr/en/head-statue-alexander-great
https://art.thewalters.org/object/23.121/
Both describe the forehead-lifted anastole; Acropolis also describes deep-set
eyes, an upward gaze and half-open lips. The Acropolis dating is disputed.
Neither a sculpture's current colour nor traces of its paint establish his
natural hair colour. Muted brown/chestnut in this card is an artistic choice.
Acropolis and Walters image downloads were unavailable during this run; those
photos were not supplied to generation. The actual visual reference was Louvre.

Art direction
Reduced hair volume and flying locks, waves closer to the skull and ears,
modest central forehead lift. Less squared lower face and exaggerated scowl,
softer cheek/jaw transitions, deep-set eyes with visible pupils and parted lips.
Retain the prior painterly chiaroscuro, mounted action and card composition.
