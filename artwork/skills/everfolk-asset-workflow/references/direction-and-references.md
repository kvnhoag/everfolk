# Direction, evidence, and review sources

Use this reference when researching or changing the character's appearance.

## Establish the image

Decide what the viewer should feel: authority, youthful ambition, menace, intellect, endurance, or another brief-specific quality. Connect that aura to silhouette, camera, pose, negative space, lighting, and one or two hero shapes. Review the silhouette and face at the intended card size before spending on detail.

Use a mature anime design language with realistic anatomical structure and observed material behaviour as the default, then painterly light/shadow rather than a photographic actor face. Chiaroscuro should model planes; it must not erase pupils or turn eyes into blank lights. Preserve distinctive reference-led facial structure. Hair volume, curls, age, and grooming depend on the character and evidence, not a universal heroic hairstyle.

When the user says a restrained weapon or closed cape lost the aura, restore the heroic silhouette and motion. Keep the reference-derived blade profile, hilt, clasp, garment cut, and other diagnostic forms where possible while intentionally exaggerating scale or movement. Record which changes are artistic. Historical plausibility is not the same claim as a museum-accurate reconstruction.

## Research real sources

Prefer museum object pages and institutional image collections for clothing, armour, swords, sculpture, and period art. Record the page URL, institution, object title, date/culture attribution, accession number if available, credit/licence, and exactly which features inform the design. Trace painterly inspiration to actual paintings and artists; identify the lighting, edge, palette, or paint handling being borrowed.

Reference pose as well as equipment. A statue, mosaic, or painting can support gesture and silhouette while leaving materials ambiguous. Do not infer a textile, metal alloy, hidden sleeve, colour, or construction from ambiguous surface shading. Name alternatives or leave the feature uncertain. Distinguish contemporaneous objects from later depictions and modern reconstructions. Respect the user's excluded research services.

Keep source files separate from generated art. Preserve the downloaded photo unchanged. If a crop aids comparison, retain the original and record the crop boundary; do not recolour, retouch, sharpen, or synthesize the reference. Label it as a crop. Do not create a fake museum photo or treat generated content as historical evidence.

## Make the reference review visible

Every result review includes actual reference photos/crops beside the candidate, not just a list of links. Add concise credit and source links next to the images, and annotate which forms were followed, what remains uncertain, and which proportions or movement were exaggerated. If a source cannot be retrieved lawfully or displayed, find an accessible authentic source or state the limitation; never fill the gap with a generated substitute.

Compare at real viewing size. Confirm that the intended aura survives pixelation, the gaze is legible, the blade and garment read clearly, and important forms are not hidden behind frame or text. Keep approved versions so later revisions can restore the stronger silhouette.
